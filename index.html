Hi! I'm Yihang Jiao, a Master of Computer Science student at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where I also received my B.S. in Mathematics and Computer Science.

I work on machine learning for biomedical data: medical image segmentation, biomedical and scientific literature retrieval, and how these models are evaluated. Evaluation is what I keep coming back to, and it feels a lot like grading a student's work — a model can reach the right answer for the wrong reason, and unless the test is built to tell the difference, the score flatters everyone.

I like the moment a pipeline becomes honest enough that a number can be traced back to the one setting that produced it. On brain tumor segmentation, that meant discovering that redesigning the loss and the sampler moved Dice about as much as changing the backbone did. In a retrieval project, it meant finding that the standard protocol handed every retriever a perfect recall@1 while those retrievers agreed on only 34.1% of their top results — the test was really asking whether a document could find itself.

Research Experience

Research Assistant, FOCAL Lab, UIUC — with Prof. Gagandeep Singh, Aug–Dec 2024.

I built a hybrid adversarial-attack framework that pairs LLaMA-2 counterfactual generation with a three-stage semantic filter (label-flip rate ≥ 0.6, embedding cosine similarity ≥ 0.75, GPT-2-XL perplexity ≤ 150), to see how classifiers hold up against attacks that rewrite a sentence rather than swap words in it. Filtering discarded 37.7% of generated candidates while keeping 91% of the effective ones, raising attack success from 68.2% to 85.1% on RoBERTa-Large. What I found most interesting is that inference-style tasks (RTE, QNLI; +11.3% on average) benefit far more from generative rewriting than sentiment tasks do, where swapping a single word is already enough.
