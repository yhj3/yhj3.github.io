Research

What I work on, and where I am trying to take it.

Interests

Machine learning for biomedical data: medical image segmentation, biomedical literature and knowledge retrieval, and reliable evaluation of deep models under class imbalance, limited supervision, and distribution shift.

The last one is not a footnote to the first two. In both of my recent projects, the most consequential finding came from the measurement rather than the model:

  • On BraTS, computing Dice only over classes actually present in a batch changed what the number meant — naive Dice is inflated by absent classes, and a model can look good on a metric that is mostly rewarding it for empty background.
  • In VibeRepair+, the field-standard leave-one-out retrieval evaluation gave every retriever a perfect recall@1 while those retrievers agreed on only 34.1% of their top-1 results. The protocol was measuring whether a document can find itself.

Biomedical settings make this worse, not better: labels are scarce and expensive, positives are rare, batch effects look exactly like signal, and the deployment distribution is rarely the training one. I want to build models for this data and the evaluation protocols that make their reported gains believable.

What I want to work on next

Evaluation protocols for machine learning on scientific data

A continuation of what I ran into in both recent projects. Leave-one-out and random-split protocols are load-bearing in a lot of published results, and they systematically overstate performance whenever the query and the gold answer share provenance — the retrievers in VibeRepair+ all scored a perfect recall@1 under a protocol that was really asking whether a document can find itself. The same shape shows up wherever the split does not hold out the thing the model is supposed to generalize over.

The deliverable I care about is a checkable recipe rather than a new model: an external-query construction, a two-stage leak check, and a random-retrieval floor reported next to every headline number. Biomedical data makes this sharper, not softer — labels are scarce, positives are rare, and batch structure correlates with condition often enough that a model can score well by reading the batch instead of the biology.

Also interested in: perturbational cell-state modeling

Predicting how a cell moves from a baseline state to a new one under a perturbation — a compound, a genetic knockout, a dose — with readouts that are heterogeneous (imaging, transcriptomics, perturbation metadata). I have not worked on this and have not thought it through in detail; I am noting it as an area I find interesting and would like to learn, not as a research plan. If you work on it, I would be glad to hear what I should be reading: yihangj3@illinois.edu.
