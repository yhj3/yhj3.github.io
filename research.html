Research

What I work on, and where I am trying to take it.

Interests

Machine learning for biomedical data: medical image segmentation, biomedical literature and knowledge retrieval, and reliable evaluation of deep models under class imbalance, limited supervision, and distribution shift.

The last one is not a footnote to the first two. In both of my recent projects, the most consequential finding came from the measurement rather than the model:

  • On BraTS, computing Dice only over classes actually present in a batch changed what the number meant — naive Dice is inflated by absent classes, and a model can look good on a metric that is mostly rewarding it for empty background.
  • In VibeRepair+, the field-standard leave-one-out retrieval evaluation gave every retriever a perfect recall@1 while those retrievers agreed on only 34.1% of their top-1 results. The protocol was measuring whether a document can find itself.

Biomedical settings make this worse, not better: labels are scarce and expensive, positives are rare, batch effects look exactly like signal, and the deployment distribution is rarely the training one. I want to build models for this data and the evaluation protocols that make their reported gains believable.

Directions I'm working toward

Multimodal perturbational cell-state modeling

In-progress direction · feedback very welcome

Virtual-cell modeling asks a prediction question I find compelling: given a cell in some baseline state and a perturbation applied to it — a compound, a genetic knockout, a dose — what state does the cell move to? The interesting version of the question is multimodal, because the readouts are heterogeneous: high-content imaging, transcriptomic profiles, and perturbation metadata each capture a different projection of the same underlying state transition, and none of them alone is the state.

Concretely, the pieces I want to work on:

  • A shared latent state across modalities. Learning a representation in which an imaging readout and a transcriptomic readout of the same perturbed condition land in the same place, so a perturbation can be modeled as an operator on that state rather than as a label attached to one assay.
  • Generalization to unseen perturbations, not just unseen cells. The hard split is held-out compounds and held-out doses, not held-out wells. Random splits over wells leak the perturbation identity, and reported performance under them mostly measures memorization — the same failure mode I found in retrieval evaluation, in a different costume.
  • Evaluation that survives batch effects. Plate and batch structure correlates with biological condition often enough that a model can score well by reading the batch. Protocols need explicit batch-held-out splits and a null model that is given only the nuisance covariates.

My existing work is the tooling for this rather than a detour from it: multi-modal volumetric data handling and imbalance-aware segmentation from BraTS, large-scale corpus construction and leak detection from the retrieval projects, and the habit of building one entrypoint where every configuration knob is recorded in the checkpoint.

This section describes work I am starting rather than results I have. If you work on virtual-cell or perturbation-response modeling and think I have a piece of it wrong, I would genuinely like to hear it — yihangj3@illinois.edu.

Evaluation protocols for retrieval-augmented scientific systems

A continuation of the VibeRepair+ line, aimed at biomedical retrieval. Leave-one-out and random-split protocols are load-bearing in a lot of published RAG results, and they systematically overstate performance whenever the query and the gold document share provenance. The deliverable I care about is a checkable recipe: an external-query construction, a two-stage leak check, and a random-retrieval floor reported alongside every headline number.
